# H3 — Proponent Brief: Round-tripping as a faithfulness oracle

**SUPPORT STRENGTH: moderate — Multiple papers (with full-text empirical results) show that back-translation/round-trip and equivalence-selection measurably improve NL→formal faithfulness and pass@1, and that verification-feedback repair loops can run with no human inner loop; BUT the strongest faithfulness gains come from *symbolic* equivalence (solver-checked), not the pure NL-round-trip leg, which is consistently the weaker signal. So round-tripping is a *useful, reliable-enough selection/repair signal*, but "sufficient on its own" is supported only when paired with a formal back-end.**

HYPOTHESIS UNDER TEST (H3): Round-tripping (render predicate→English and compare to the source claim; or back-translate and check equivalence) is a SUFFICIENT, RELIABLE oracle for NL→predicate faithfulness, enabling a generate-verify-repair loop without a human inner loop.

This brief marshals the strongest *honest* case FOR H3. Where evidence is abstract-only it is tagged `[ABSTRACT]`; full-text-grounded claims are tagged `[FULL TEXT]`.

---

## 1. The core mechanism is demonstrated and quantified: back-translation + equivalence-selection improves pass@1

**Li et al., "Autoformalize Mathematical Statements by Symbolic Equivalence and Semantic Consistency", NeurIPS 2024. DOI: 10.48550/arxiv.2410.20936** `[FULL TEXT]`

This is the single strongest paper for H3 because it isolates and measures *exactly* the round-trip mechanism the hypothesis names.

- **Mechanism (semantic consistency = round-trip):** "An autoformalization result is accurate if it can be re-informalized to a version consistent with the original statement in natural language." They formalize this as **τ-semantic consistency**: take candidate formalization Ψ, informalize it back to natural language Ψ̃, and accept if the embedding similarity Sim(Ψ̃, Φ) ≥ τ against the original NL statement Φ (Definition 2). This is precisely H3's "back-translate and check equivalence."
- **Why it works:** "informalization is much easier and accurate than formalization", so the round-trip error is dominated by the formalization step — i.e., the round-trip is a *sensitive* probe of formalization error, not swamped by back-translation noise.
- **It catches errors symbolic checks miss:** a trivially-true formalization ("4 = 4") is symbolically equivalent to the correct one but "ruins the semantics in the informal statement", so semantic consistency rejects it. Round-trip is therefore *complementary* to solver checks and catches a distinct failure class (vacuity / meaning-drift).
- **Quantified faithfulness/pass@1 gains:** Selecting among k candidates with these self-consistency oracles closes the documented pass@1↔pass@10 gap (19.5–26.5%). The combined oracle yields up to **7.8% (MATH) / 10.7% (miniF2F)** improvement over baseline with GPT-4, and 0.22–1.35× relative improvement across five LLMs. Pure semantic-consistency (SemCo, the round-trip-only leg) beats the baseline ranking by **0.8–3.9% in 1@k** and wins 4/5 models vs. alternatives.
- **Labeling-efficiency (the "without a human inner loop" payoff):** the method gives 8.4–21.9% relative reduction in human verification/labeling effort, "efficiently minimizing human intervention in correcting and validating outputs."

> Strength note: This is full-text, multi-model, two-dataset (MATH, miniF2F), with an automatic oracle for miniF2F. It directly supports both halves of H3 — that round-trip improves faithfulness AND reduces human-in-the-loop cost. It also supplies the honest ceiling (see §5).

## 2. Sub-formula back-mapping makes round-trip *actionable* and supports a no-human repair step

**Cosler et al., "nl2spec: Interactively Translating Unstructured Natural Language to Temporal Logics with LLMs", CAV 2023. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-37703-7_18** `[FULL TEXT]`

nl2spec's core contribution is a finer-grained round-trip: "we utilize LLMs to map subformulas of the formalization back to the corresponding natural language fragments of the input" (sub-translations), each with a confidence score computed by majority vote over multiple runs.

- **Round-trip improves quality even with no human:** In the **teacher-student experiment**, sub-translations generated by a strong model (Codex) were fed to a weak model (Bloom). On 11 instances Codex solved but Bloom initially could not, Bloom solved 4 more (36.4%) — and crucially: *"no supervision by a human was needed in this experiment to improve the formalization quality."* This is direct evidence that the back-mapping signal alone (not a human) drives faithfulness up.
- **Confidence via self-consistency:** confidence scores are "votes over multiple queries", i.e., an automatable self-consistency oracle for sub-translations.
- **Repair is cheap and convergent:** fixing erroneous translations took only **1.86 loops on average**; overall accuracy rose from 44.4% (initial) to 86.1% via sub-translation editing. While the headline study used a human, the mechanism (decompose → back-map → score → re-prompt) is exactly the inner loop H3 proposes, and the teacher-student result shows it functions autonomously.

> Strength note: full-text, real user-study dataset (36 expert-curated hard LTL instances). Supports H3's *decomposed* round-trip and the convergent repair loop; the no-human evidence is one controlled sub-experiment rather than the main evaluation.

## 3. Verification-feedback repair loops with NO human inner loop are demonstrated end-to-end

These papers instantiate H3's "generate-verify-repair loop without a human inner loop" using *formal* equivalence/verification as the oracle instead of (or alongside) NL similarity — the most reliable instantiation of round-tripping.

**SpecLoop: An Agentic RTL-to-Specification Framework with Formal Verification Feedback Loop** `[ABSTRACT]` (url: semanticscholar.org/paper/ba99a2f5e9e57963c2005449c674d33d0e4cdaae)
- This is round-tripping in its purest auditable form: "generates candidate specifications and then **reconstructs RTL from these specifications**; it uses formal equivalence checking tools between the reconstructed RTL and the original design to validate functional consistency. When mismatches are detected, counterexamples are fed back to iteratively refine the specifications until equivalence is proven." That is *exactly* generate → round-trip → equivalence-check → repair, with the loop closed by a sound checker rather than a human.
- Result: "incorporating formal verification feedback substantially improves specification correctness and robustness over LLM-only baselines."

**PAT-Agent: Autoformalization for Model Checking, ASE 2025. DOI: 10.1109/ase63991.2025.00176** `[ABSTRACT]`
- End-to-end NL→formal model with a **Repair Loop**: generated models are verified by the PAT model checker against properties, and "when discrepancies occur, a Repair Loop is triggered to iteratively correct the model using counterexamples." Ablations "confirm the importance of both planning and repair components." On 40 systems it "consistently outperforms baselines." Demonstrates a counterexample-driven, no-human inner repair loop raising faithfulness.

**Agentic Model Checking / BMC-Agent** `[ABSTRACT]` (url: semanticscholar.org/paper/dbb4a89d5e0e7f847f0c7f679d9fbcc9994eeffd)
- Principle "agents propose, solvers verify": LLM agents handle spec inference and refinement proposals while bounded model checking "discharges every soundness-relevant decision." Counterexamples drive a refinement loop automatically. This is the architecture H3 needs — the oracle (BMC) is sound, the inner loop is agentic, no human gates each iteration.

**Towards Autoformalization of LLM-generated Outputs for Requirement Verification, 2025. arXiv:2511.11829** `[ABSTRACT]`
- A simple LLM autoformalizer verified LLM outputs against NL requirements: it "successfully identified that two differently-worded NL requirements were logically equivalent" and "identified a logical inconsistency between a given NL requirement and an LLM-generated output." Direct, if small, evidence that autoformalize-then-equivalence-check works as a consistency oracle.

> Strength note: SpecLoop and PAT-Agent are the cleanest end-to-end demonstrations of H3's loop, but both are abstract-only here, and both close the loop with a *formal* checker (equivalence/model-checking) rather than pure NL round-trip. They support "generate-verify-repair without a human inner loop" strongly; they support "NL round-trip alone is the oracle" only by analogy.

## 4. The general principle is well-established: a formal/executable selection oracle beats textual scoring

**Pan et al., "Logic-LM", Findings of EMNLP 2023. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2023.findings-emnlp.248** `[FULL TEXT]` and **Olausson et al., "LINC", EMNLP 2023. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.313** `[FULL TEXT]`
- Both show that translating NL→formal and then using a symbolic back-end to *select/verify* yields large faithfulness/accuracy gains over LLM-only reasoning, and that solver feedback can drive self-refinement. These corroborate the general H3 thesis — that a checkable round-trip/verification signal, not human judgement, is what improves faithfulness — even though their back-end is a logic solver rather than NL back-translation.

**Wu et al., "Autoformalization with Large Language Models", NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2205.12615** `[FULL TEXT]`
- Establishes the empirical asymmetry that underpins round-tripping: informalization is *more accurate* than formalization (76% vs 25% correct), so back-translation is a comparatively trustworthy half of the round-trip. The authors explicitly point to "the prospect of backtranslation style algorithms" and "cycle-consistency-based" loops as the path forward.

## 5. Honest scope and the strongest counter-nuance (folded in)

To keep this brief honest, the boundaries where "SUFFICIENT and RELIABLE on its own" is *not* supported:

- **Round-trip via NL similarity is the weaker leg.** In Li et al. (§1), pure semantic consistency (SemCo) is "much less effective than SymEq in most cases, as it does not grasp the logical nature of formal statements." The big wins come from *combining* round-trip with solver-checked symbolic equivalence (log-comb sweet spot α≈0.32–0.6). So round-tripping is reliable as a *complementary/selection* signal, weaker as a *standalone* faithfulness oracle.
- **Textual-similarity round-trip can miss logical strength/vacuity.** **Lahiri, "Evaluating LLM-driven User-Intent Formalization for Verification-Aware Languages", FMCAD 2024. arXiv:2406.09757** `[FULL TEXT]` argues semantic-equivalence-only metrics are "too strict" yet textual-similarity metrics (BLEU) used by autoformalization "do not use semantic checks (such as tests and symbolic verification)"; it advocates *symbolic testing against I/O examples + mutation* and shows this automated metric "agrees closely" with human labels — and even catches cases where human STRONG_SPEC labels were wrong. Read FOR H3: this strengthens the loop-without-a-human claim (an automated oracle can match/exceed human labeling), but it relocates the oracle from NL round-trip to *symbolic* checking. It is the key reason the standalone "sufficient" claim is only moderately, not strongly, supported.
- **Equivalence is the right relation, but checking it is the hard part.** Trustworthy Formal NL Specifications (Gordon & Matskevich, Onward! 2023) shows the *gold standard* is a machine-checked logical-equivalence certificate between NL and formal claim — achievable when you control the grammar, but it is auditable equivalence, not LLM round-trip. It bounds how reliable an LLM round-trip can be expected to be without a certificate.

---

## Bottom line for H3
Round-tripping/back-translation/equivalence-selection is empirically shown to (a) improve NL→formal faithfulness and pass@1 (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024; nl2spec teacher-student), and (b) power generate-verify-repair loops that run with **no human in the inner loop** (SpecLoop, PAT-Agent, BMC-Agent). It is therefore a **reliable and useful** oracle. The "**sufficient on its own**" clause holds best when the round-trip is closed by a *formal* equivalence/verification check (symbolic equivalence, model checking, or symbolic testing); the pure NL-similarity round-trip is reliable as a selection/repair signal but is consistently the weaker leg and can miss logical vacuity/strength. Hence: **moderate (trending strong) support.**
