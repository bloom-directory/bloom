# Secure natural-language → on-chain invariant generation: can it be made trustworthy, and what representation should be the shared referent?

> **TL;DR:** NL→predicate generation is real and useful, but its *security does not come from generation* — it comes from a **sound, deterministic checker** that disposes of the generator's output. That checker certifies **form, not meaning**: the predicate-vs-intent gap is *narrowable* by deterministic proxies (vacuity, mutation, equivalence, ensembles) but **not fully closable** by any automated or on-chain mechanism, so an irreducible residual lands on human review / social arbitration. NL round-trip ("render the predicate back to English") is a *readability aid, not a faithfulness gate*. For a blockchain specifically, the on-chain gate must **establish** universal safety via a deterministically-checked predicate per transition (which Bloom has); LLM-generated **witnesses can only refute**, and belong off-chain. Confidence: moderate–high on the off-chain claims (well-evidenced), tentative on the on-chain specifics (extrapolated — the corpus barely addresses consensus determinism).

## What We Found

**1. Security comes from the gate, not the generator — but the gate certifies form, not meaning.** (V-001, *Supported, refined*; moderate–high) A sound deterministic disposing step is what makes an unreliable generator usable: LINC's prover gate lets a 15.5B model beat GPT‑4+CoT, while its Scratchpad ablation (LLM does the deduction) erases the gain *(full text)* [LINC, 10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.313]; symbolic-equivalence selection over k candidates recovers a large pass@1→pass@k gap *(full text)* [Autoformalize by Symbolic Equivalence and Semantic Consistency, arXiv:2410.20936]; Logic-LM is "faithful as long as the formulation is correct," with a deterministic solver doing inference *(full text)* [Logic-LM, 10.18653/v1/2023.findings-emnlp.248]. **But** the same full texts show the gate guarantees *soundness relative to the formal artifact*, not intent — it will pass a vacuous "4=4" or `true` [Lahiri, *Evaluating LLM-driven User-Intent Formalization*, arXiv:2406.09757 (full text); Logic-LM §4.4]. The honest correction the Falsifier forced: gains *also* track generator scale and the gate's lift vanishes for the strongest generator (LINC on FOLIO, p=0.58), so "security is *independent* of the generator" is overstated.

**2. The predicate-vs-intent gap is narrowable but not closable.** (V-004, *Supported, refined*; high) Deterministic proxies measurably narrow it: an automated mutation + vacuity metric agrees closely with human intent labels (and even *corrects* mislabeled ones) [Lahiri, arXiv:2406.09757 (full text)]; ensembles [LINC (full text)], round-trip + symbolic equivalence [arXiv:2410.20936 (full text)], and retrieval-augmented property generation finding real zero-days [PropertyGPT, 10.14722/ndss.2025.241357 (full text)] all help. **But** every proxy is oracle-relative and residual NL ambiguity needs a human — "the only bridge … is the humans" [Trustworthy Formal Natural Language Specifications, arXiv:2310.03885 (full text)]; nl2spec is interactive by design [CAV 2023 (full text)]. So intent-conformance has an irreducible residual that no in-pipeline (hence no on-chain) check removes.

**3. NL round-trip / back-translation is not a faithfulness gate.** (V-003, *Refuted as stated*; high) Same-model forward/back translation shares failure modes (self-consistency ≠ faithfulness); equivalence-against-a-reference is explicitly rejected as a faithfulness metric because it "cannot distinguish weak and vacuous specifications from strong yet incomplete" ones [Lahiri, arXiv:2406.09757 (full text)]; the back-translation leg "does not grasp the logical nature of formal statements" [arXiv:2410.20936 (full text)]. What *is* reliable is a **formal** check (equivalence/execution/witness), not NL paraphrase. (Scoped per RT-002: cross-model or formally-closed round-trips do add value; NL similarity alone does not.)

**4. Restriction buys determinism, not fidelity.** (V-002, *Refuted as stated*; high) The hypothesis that one mechanism — grammar restriction — *jointly* lowers intent error and supplies on-chain determinism fails. A controlled-NL grammar by itself produced semantically *wrong* English; fidelity returned only when formal semantics were injected, at which point readability collapsed [Developing controlled NL for formal specification patterns (full text)]. PropertyGPT deliberately chose an *expressive* property language over the restricted Certora CVL and won on real DeFi (80% recall, 12 zero-days) [NDSS 2025 (full text)]. DeCon had to drop recursion and cross-contract calls to stay decidable [Safety Verification of Declarative Smart Contracts (full text)]. Determinism/decidable-equivalence and intent-fidelity are **separable**; the restricted representation secures the former (real and valuable) but is neither sufficient nor free for the latter.

**5. On-chain, witnesses refute; checked predicates establish.** (V-005, *Supported, refined*; high on the asymmetry, tentative on the on-chain prescription) The refute-vs-establish asymmetry is stated by the source authors: an LLM-proposed, concretely-executed violation witness gives 100%-precision *refutation* "by construction," but universal/liveness shapes are "more similar to a proof than a counterexample" and are *not expressible* [Neuroforger, arXiv:2605.31389 (full text)]. Witnesses are also non-deterministic / environment-dependent, so they cannot sit in a consensus gate. Establishing a universal safety invariant requires induction over all reachable states [DeCon (full text)] — whose on-chain analogue is a deterministically-checked predicate evaluated every transition.

## The Novel Insight

**The thing that must be the same object for all three parties is a *token*, not a *type* — and that is why the gate can never close the intent gap.** Across the corpus, every reliable mechanism works by reducing a question to *checking a fixed syntactic object*: the solver checks *this* formula, the witness replays *this* trace, the mutation score probes *this* predicate. That is type-level identity — the predicate as a canonical, deterministically-evaluable object — and it is exactly what a consensus gate and an arbitration referent need. **But "intent" is not a syntactic object at all; it is a token-level relation between a predicate and a situation in a human's head.** No amount of work on the shared *type* (better grammar, round-trip, equivalence) reaches the *token* (did this predicate mean what the author meant *here*), which is why Lahiri can prove "no algorithmic way" and simultaneously *narrow* the gap with proxies: proxies sharpen the type; they cannot supply the token. This reframes the whole pipeline: the deterministic machinery's job is to make the predicate a **maximally legible, canonical type** (decidable, total, back-renderable) so that the *human's* token-level judgment — the only place intent lives — is as cheap and reliable as possible. Security is therefore a *division of labor by what is mechanizable*, not a gate you can finish building. This both vindicates Bloom's quarantined predicate-AST (ADR-001) as the right *type*, and explains why its deferred faithfulness gate (ADR-003) can only ever be an *off-chain aid to human token-judgment*, never an on-chain guarantee — matching the project's own instinct to keep the predicate authoritative and `text` inert.

## Recommendation for Bloom (engineering inference — tentative, see RT-003)

1. **Keep the predicate AST as the authoritative, on-chain-enforced type** (decidable, total, deterministic). This is the corpus-supported sweet spot for the *type* role (V-002 surviving leg, insight).
2. **Treat NL→predicate generation as legitimate but off-chain, and never trusted directly** — route it through the deterministic deploy gates you already have (enforceability, field-ref, fuel, vacuity, boundary). "Agents propose, the deterministic gate disposes" (V-001).
3. **Do not promote `render_predicate_english`/`text` to a faithfulness gate** (V-003). It is a legibility aid that lowers the cost of human token-judgment; keep it inert to enforcement, as it is today.
4. **Realize the deferred ADR-003 faithfulness work off-chain**, as a *narrowing* aid (vacuity ✓ already, mutation score, ensemble/cross-model consistency, spec test-vectors), explicitly leaving an irreducible residual to human review / the verification market — never an LLM-in-consensus (V-004).
5. **Add a witness/counterexample *challenge* path off-chain** (V-005): an LLM emits a candidate violating transaction; the chain's deterministic re-execution validates it. This *refutes* a deployed invariant or contract bug trustlessly, complementing — not replacing — the per-transition checked predicate that *establishes* safety.

## Open Questions
**The single most valuable open question:** *Is there a deterministic, consensus-reproducible proxy whose agreement with human intent-judgment is high enough to be worth pinning on-chain (e.g. a `text_hash` bound to a mutation/vacuity/boundary score), or does every such proxy stay strictly off-chain?* It matters because it is exactly the line between what Bloom can put in consensus and what must remain social — and the corpus, which barely touches the consensus-determinism constraint (RT-003), cannot answer it. A focused study: take real DeFi invariants, hold the predicate fixed, and measure how well deterministic proxies predict expert intent-faithfulness versus how well a (non-deterministic) LLM judge does.
