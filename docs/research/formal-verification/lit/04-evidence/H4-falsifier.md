# H4 Falsifier — "A conformance profile + test-vector suite (not a binary) is SUFFICIENT for cross-node deterministic Wasm execution"

REFUTATION STRENGTH: partial — The corpus supplies a strong *mechanistic* and a strong *authority* argument that a finite test-vector suite under-determines consensus-critical Wasm semantics and that the real sufficiency condition is a *pinned verified executable semantics* (which ADR-005 itself names only as a "long-term oracle," not a present requirement). But the one paper that would make the refutation **decisive** — the differential-fuzzing demonstration of conformant-yet-divergent VMs — is present in the corpus as **title/metadata only, with an empty abstract**, so its decisive disconfirming result cannot be verified from the evidence available. Refutation is therefore strong-but-not-decisive.

Hypothesis under attack: ADR-005 ("Pin a conformance profile, not a build hash") decides that a *versioned conformance profile* — feature set, canonical fuel schedule, disabled nondeterministic features — "enforced by a test-vector suite an engine build must pass to be consensus-eligible" is what makes two honest nodes agree, with "WasmCert/WasmRef as the long-term oracle." H4 promotes this to a **sufficiency** claim. "Sufficient" is a strong claim: it asserts that passing the finite suite *entails* bit-identical execution on every consensus-reachable input. A single conformant-but-divergent engine pair refutes it. The decision text's own hedge — verified semantics demoted to "long-term" — is the seam I attack.

---

## 1. The sampling gap is structural, not incidental (mechanistic refutation)

A test-vector suite is a finite sample of an infinite input space; conformance on the sample does not entail agreement off the sample. The corpus shows this gap is real and exploited in practice.

- **WasmRef-Isabelle: A Verified Monadic Interpreter and Industrial Fuzzing Oracle for WebAssembly** (DOI 10.1145/3591224; OpenAlex W4379512473) (abstract only; full-text fetch failed, 403). The decisive structural fact is in the abstract: WasmRef-Isabelle "has been adopted and deployed as a fuzzing oracle in the continuous integration infrastructure of Wasmtime, a widely used WebAssembly implementation." You do not deploy a *differential fuzzing oracle* against an engine that already passes the official conformance suite unless conformance is **known to be an insufficient guarantee of correct semantics**. The entire premise of WasmRef-Isabelle's industrial deployment is that Wasmtime — a flagship, fully conformant engine — still has reachable inputs on which its behavior must be continuously checked against a *semantics*, not against a test list. This is the operational refutation of sufficiency: the industry's own answer to "does conformance guarantee correct execution?" is "no — we run a verified oracle in CI to keep finding the cases the suite misses."

- This generalizes the sampling argument from a logical truism to an empirically load-bearing one: if a finite suite were sufficient, fuzzing the conformant engine against a verified oracle would find nothing and would not be worth deploying in CI. The fact that it *is* deployed means the residual divergence surface after conformance is non-empty and economically worth hunting. For a blockchain, any single such residual divergence between two honest, conformant nodes is a latent consensus fork.

## 2. The real sufficiency condition is a pinned verified semantics — which ADR-005 declines to require now

The corpus shows that even the artifacts a profile would lean on (the reference interpreter, the prose spec) have themselves been *wrong* or *inadequate*, so a profile that points at "the spec / the reference interpreter" under-determines semantics unless a verified executable semantics is the binding oracle.

- **WasmRef-Isabelle** (DOI 10.1145/3591224) (abstract only), again, contains the sharpest admission: "Previous efforts to fuzz Wasmtime against WebAssembly's official OCaml reference interpreter were abandoned by Wasmtime's developers after the reference interpreter exhibited unacceptable performance characteristics, which its maintainers decided not to fix in order to preserve the interpreter's close definitional correspondence with the official specification." Two consequences for H4: (a) the *official reference interpreter* — the most natural concrete pin for a "profile" — was found unusable as an oracle and dropped; (b) the replacement that the industry actually trusts is one "proven correct with respect to the WasmCert-Isabelle mechanisation," i.e., a **verified executable semantics**. The sufficiency that delivers cross-engine agreement in practice is being supplied by the *verified semantics*, not by the test suite. ADR-005 names exactly this artifact (WasmCert/WasmRef) but files it under "long-term oracle," while claiming present sufficiency for the profile-plus-suite alone. The corpus says the oracle is doing the load-bearing work *today*.

- **Mechanising and verifying the WebAssembly specification** (DOI 10.1145/3167082; OpenAlex W2778960843) (abstract only; full-text fetch failed). The abstract states the working group's formal efforts "so far have focussed on pen and paper formal specification" — and the paper's purpose is to *mechanise* it, which (per the SpecTec full text below) surfaced corrections to that pen-and-paper spec. The implication for H4: a profile written against the prose/pen-and-paper specification inherits whatever was wrong or under-specified in it; only a *mechanised* (machine-checked) executable semantics removes that under-determination. A test-vector suite cannot, even in principle, certify the *un-sampled* region; a verified semantics can, by construction, decide every input. This is the difference between "passes the cases we wrote" and "agrees with the meaning of the language." Sufficiency requires the latter.

- **Iris-Wasm: Robust and Modular Verification of WebAssembly Programs** (DOI 10.1145/3591265; OpenAlex W4379537472) (abstract only; full-text fetch failed). Corroborates that the trustworthy guarantees about Wasm behavior in the literature are obtained by building "on a specification of Wasm 1.0 mechanized in Coq" and proving properties against it — again a *mechanized semantics* as the source of assurance, never a test suite. The whole verification ecosystem the corpus documents is organized around pinning semantics, not enumerating vectors. (Weight: indirect — Iris-Wasm targets module isolation/functional correctness, not engine differential conformance, but it confirms where the field locates ground truth.)

## 3. SpecTec confirms under-specified / implementation-defined corners a test suite would not pin down

- **Wasm SpecTec: Engineering a Formal Language Standard** (DOI 10.48550/arxiv.2311.07223; OpenAlex W4388685487) (full text). SpecTec is the corpus's clearest window into *how the standard is actually engineered*, and it both helps and hurts H4 — read carefully it hurts more than it helps.

  - It states the four mandated artifacts — formal spec, prose, reference interpreter, and unit-test suite — exist precisely to "reduce the risk of implementation divergence," and that the test suite "guarantees that various implementations of Wasm exhibit consistent behavior" (full text, §1). On its face this *supports* H4. But the same section concedes the failure mode H4 must rule out: divergence "may occur inadvertently simply due to the number of different browser implementations in existence, each one in itself consisting of several tiers of interpretation and just-in-time compilation." That is exactly Bloom's situation — multiple independent engine builds, each with interpreter + JIT tiers — and SpecTec describes inadvertent divergence as an *expected* hazard the artifacts only "reduce the risk of," never eliminate. "Reduces the risk of divergence" is not "is sufficient to guarantee identical results." The authority Bloom would cite for sufficiency explicitly disclaims sufficiency and offers only risk reduction.

  - SpecTec's motivation is that the hand-authored spec artifacts are error-prone: authors "complain of … no protection against misuse of definitions (e.g. wrong arguments, incorrect placement, incorrect symbol bindings)," and the whole tool exists because "manually crafting all of these artefacts requires expert knowledge combined with repetitive and tedious labor" that has produced lagging, error-prone artifacts (full text, §1). If the *specification itself* is error-prone enough to need automated meta-checking and a single source of truth, then a profile + test suite derived from it under-determines semantics in exactly the corners where the hand-authored spec is wrong or silent.

  - Crucially, SpecTec's own validation is **test-suite based and was not exhaustive**: "the extracted semantics passed all 23,778 tests (SIMD excluded) in the test suite" (full text, §2, §4). SIMD was excluded; the suite is a fixed, enumerable set. Passing 23,778 fixed vectors is the *ceiling* of what conformance testing certifies — and it says nothing about the un-enumerated remainder or the excluded feature set. This is the sampling gap made concrete in the corpus's own numbers: a famous, carefully engineered semantics is validated by *counting* the vectors it passes, which is precisely the methodology H4 claims is sufficient and which the WasmRef-Isabelle deployment (§1) demonstrates is not.

## 4. The decisive disconfirming result: present in the corpus by name only

- **Uncovering Smart Contract VM Bugs Via Differential Fuzzing** (Maier, Fäßler, Seifert; ROOTS 2021; DOI 10.1145/3503921.3503923) (metadata/title only — **abstract empty and no full text in corpus**). This is the paper that would make the refutation decisive: differential fuzzing of independent smart-contract VM implementations is the exact experimental setup for finding *conformant-but-divergent* engines, i.e., a directly observed latent consensus fork. The title, venue (Reversing and Offensive-oriented Trends Symposium), and the authorship strongly indicate the paper *does* report such divergences across VM implementations. **But I cannot cite findings I cannot read.** The corpus entry carries no abstract and no full text, so the specific claim — "independent VMs that pass conformance yet diverge on adversarial inputs" — is *expected but unverified* from this evidence corpus. Honest scientific practice forbids grading the refutation "decisive" on the strength of a paper reduced to its title. I flag this as the single highest-value gap to close: obtaining this paper's results section would likely convert this falsification from partial to decisive.

- **Detecting nondeterministic payment bugs in Ethereum smart contracts** (DOI 10.1145/3360615; OpenAlex W2979467439) (abstract only; full-text fetch failed). Adjacent corroboration that consensus-critical nondeterminism is real, common, and pattern-evading: NPChecker found nondeterministic payments in 1,111 of 3,075 distinct mainnet contracts, rooted in "read-write hazards because of unpredictable transaction scheduling and external callee behavior" — sources "not yet fully understood" and "missed by existing research relying on a 'contract vulnerability checklist.'" A test-vector suite *is* a vulnerability/behavior checklist; the paper's central finding is that checklists miss the live nondeterminism. (Caveat: these are *contract-level / environmental* nondeterminism bugs on the EVM, not *engine-level* Wasm semantic divergence, so this supports the meta-claim "finite checklists miss consensus-critical nondeterminism" rather than the precise Wasm-engine divergence H4 needs. Weight: supporting, not direct.)

## What survives / what I could not refute

H4's sufficiency claim does **not** survive as stated, for two corpus-grounded reasons that do not depend on the unreadable differential-fuzzing paper:

1. **Operational (decisive-grade within its scope):** The industry deploys a *verified* fuzzing oracle (WasmRef-Isabelle) in the CI of a fully conformant engine (Wasmtime) — an action that is only rational if conformance is known to be insufficient to guarantee correct/identical semantics. The corpus shows the actual sufficiency carrier is the verified semantics, which ADR-005 demotes to "long-term."
2. **Authoritative:** SpecTec, the standard's own engineering paper, describes the artifact bundle (including the test suite) as *reducing the risk of* — not eliminating — inadvertent divergence among multi-tier engine implementations, and validates even its showcase semantics by counting a fixed 23,778-vector suite with SIMD excluded.

What I could **not** establish, and why the grade is *partial not decisive*:
- The corpus contains **no readable demonstration** of two independently-implemented, conformance-passing engines actually diverging on an adversarial input. The differential-fuzzing paper that would supply it is title-only. Without it, the refutation rests on (a) the logical sampling argument, (b) the strong-but-circumstantial WasmRef-Isabelle deployment rationale, and (c) SpecTec's own hedging — a powerful case, but not a single quoted counterexample of conformant-yet-divergent engines.

Net: ADR-005 is more defensible if reframed from H4's "profile + suite is *sufficient*" to "profile + suite is *necessary but not sufficient*; cross-node determinism requires the pinned verified executable semantics (WasmCert/WasmRef) as the binding consensus oracle, with the test suite as a fast pre-filter." The corpus supports that necessity-of-the-oracle reading and contradicts the sufficiency-of-the-suite reading — but, lacking the decisive differential-fuzzing result in readable form, stops short of decisively refuting the sufficiency claim.
